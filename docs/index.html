Welcome inside a real human head 🧠

Everything you see was reconstructed from a single MRI scan of a real person: the skin, the skull, the brain with its folds, the tiny inner ear and even the veins. Nothing here is a generic textbook model.

Getting around

  • Rotate: drag with the left mouse button (or one finger)
  • Move: drag with the right mouse button (or two fingers)
  • Zoom: mouse wheel or pinch
  • Learn: hover over any part to see its name and what it does; click it to keep the description on screen

Start with the guided views

The buttons at the top of the menu set up interesting scenes, like a see-through “glass head”, the brain split in half, or a virtual cut through the middle. Then play with the sliders and checkboxes.

Tools

  • Volume rendering shows the raw scan as a 3D cloud. Move Threshold to peel away the faint layers.
  • Cut slices the model open; ⇄ flips which half is removed.
  • Ruler: click two points to measure in millimetres. Esc clears it.
  • Split brain pulls the two hemispheres apart.

This is a visualization for curiosity and education, not a diagnosis. Automatic segmentation makes mistakes.

How this was made

A routine brain MRI (3 Tesla Siemens Skyra, 23 series) was downloaded from a patient portal as DICOM, anonymized, and processed with open-source research tools:

  • Brain regions: FastSurfer (deep-learning segmentation, DKT atlas) on the 0.5 mm T1 scan
  • Skull & eyes: SimNIBS charm (atlas-based head segmentation) on T1 + T2
  • Skin: thresholding of the T1 scan
  • Inner ear: fluid in the 0.3 mm T2 SPACE scan
  • Veins: Frangi vesselness filter on the SWI scan
  • Surfaces: marching cubes, smoothing and decimation, about 3 million triangles in total

The viewer is a single HTML file using three.js. Source code, data and the full methodology are on GitHub.

Honest limitations

  • Bone is dark on MRI, so the skull comes from an atlas-guided estimate, not a direct measurement.
  • Some vein fragments near the skull base are artefacts caused by air in the sinuses.
  • Automatic labels can be off by a few millimetres at region borders.
